Activities

Outside of coursework, most of my time goes toward two things: programs that push me to think differently and sports.

Engineering Honors EDGE

I was selected as one of 35 students for Engineering Honors EDGE, which stands for Enhancing the Development of Global Engineers. The program brings together students from different majors to work through real engineering and technology challenges with industry professionals. The most valuable part of EDGE is that it forces you to work outside your own field. As a computer science student, working alongside mechanical engineers and other engineering disciplines on the same problem teaches you how to communicate across disciplines and think about problems from angles you would not naturally consider on your own. That kind of cross-disciplinary thinking is something classroom assignments rarely replicate.

Aggies in Tech

I was also selected as one of 40 students for Aggies in Tech, a Business of Technology certification program at Texas A&M. The program focuses on the overlap between technology and business, covering topics like product strategy, go-to-market planning, and how companies evaluate and adopt new technologies. Coming from a pure computer science background, having formal exposure to the business side of tech has been genuinely useful. Understanding why a product gets built, who it is built for, and how it reaches the market is just as important as knowing how to build it.

Sports & Athletics

Outside of school I play flag football and basketball in intramural leagues on campus, follow the NFL and NBA closely, and spend a lot of time thinking about sports data and how teams use analytics to make better decisions. That interest in sports analytics is part of what led me to build the NFL prediction model in my portfolio section. Intramural sports have also taught me a lot about teamwork: showing up for your teammates, communicating on the field, and staying positive when a game is not going your way.

Running

Running is something I do consistently as well, usually a few times a week. It is one of the better ways I have found to clear my head between long stretches of coding. It also takes discipline to keep it up week after week, and that same consistency carries over into how I handle long projects.
